# Definition

**Provisional thesis definition:** persistent, governed knowledge and experience that outlive individual agent instances and can support later collective work. The supplied sources motivate this definition through agentic memory and long-horizon state; neither establishes this as a validated organizational-memory theory. [@alaanzy2026autonomous, PDF p. 19, §6.3; A04] [@zhu2026orchestration, PDF pp. 27–28, 47–48, §§7.1.1, 10.7; Z05]

# Technical Explanation

The proposed distinction is institutional reuse: retention needs ownership, provenance, access policy, revision history and criteria for accepting lessons. A shared transcript is only one possible input, not sufficient evidence of organizational learning. [@alaanzy2026autonomous, PDF p. 19, §6.3; A04] [@zhu2026orchestration, PDF pp. 27–28, 47–48, §§7.1.1, 10.7; Z05]

# Relevant Architectures and Mechanisms

Source mechanisms include cross-episode agentic memory, selective shared state and persistence of agent rosters and policies. Proposed additions are evidence-linked knowledge records, conflict-preserving revisions and an admission process for reusable lessons. [@alaanzy2026autonomous, PDF p. 19, §6.3; A04] [@zhu2026orchestration, PDF pp. 27–28, 47–48, §§7.1.1, 10.7; Z05]

# Differences in Interpretation

Ala’anzy identifies weak validation of self-revising memory. Zhu identifies memory–coordination coupling and cross-session drift. They do not evaluate the combined governance mechanism proposed here. [@alaanzy2026autonomous, PDF p. 19, §6.3; A04] [@zhu2026orchestration, PDF pp. 27–28, 47–48, §§7.1.1, 10.7; Z05]

# Relevance to the Thesis

**Analyst synthesis / proposed design:** This is a candidate contribution boundary: demonstrate whether governed reuse across heterogeneous, replaceable agents improves continuity while limiting stale or erroneous knowledge propagation.
